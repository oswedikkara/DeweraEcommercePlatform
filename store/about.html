Chapter I • Historic Provenance

Ratna Dweepa: 2,500 Years of Sovereign Gems

Since antiquity, the island of Sri Lanka was recorded in Sanskrit chronicles as Ratna Dweepa, the Island of Precious Gems. King Solomon dispatched merchant ships to Ceylon to procure royal sapphires and rubies for the Queen of Sheba. Marco Polo wrote of the island's majestic crystals in 1292, and the British Imperial State Crown and royal engagement jewels have honored Ceylon corundum for generations.

Dewera operates exclusively within Sri Lanka's sovereign gemfields Ratnapura (The City of Gems), Balangoda, Pelmadulla, Elahera, and Okkampitiya. We source crystals directly from alluvial riverbeds (*illam* gravel strata) where ancient metamorphic rivers naturally tumbled and concentrated the rarest crystals on Earth.

Chapter II • Thermal Metallurgy

The Science of Natural Heat

In nature, corundum crystallizes deep within the earth's crust under extreme geothermal temperatures and intense lithospheric pressure. However, many stones emerge with microscopic inclusions of rutile silk (titanium dioxide needles) that diffuse light and subdue natural color.

For centuries, Sri Lankan master burners have practiced high temperature wood charcoal and crucible heat enhancement (1,600°C to 1,850°C). This traditional thermal protocol completes what nature began: the rutile needles dissolve back into the crystal matrix, freeing titanium and iron atoms to create velvety electric royal blues, canary pushparaga golds, and lotus padparadscha tones.

The Dewera Purity Guarantee: We reject chemical diffusion (beryllium, titanium coating), cobalt glass filling, polymer resins, and radiation. Our stones are certified 100% natural heated permanent, stable, and untampered.
Chapter III • Environmental Custody

Artisanal Ethics & Alluvial Stewardship

Unlike high impact open pit strip mines that leave irreversible scars on the environment, Sri Lanka's gem industry operates under strict legal and ecological preservation frameworks. Dewera supports traditional artisanal shaft and pit mining that utilizes timber framing, hand baskets, and natural water channels.

Once an alluvial deposit has been recovered, the soil layers are methodically restored and replanted with native vegetation and tea crops. This zero strip mining ethos ensures that Sri Lanka's lush rainforest canopies, river basins, and surrounding communities remain pristine for future generations.
